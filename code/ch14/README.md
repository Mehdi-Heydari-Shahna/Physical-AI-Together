# Chapter 14: arms and heavy-duty mobile manipulators

Section 14.1 takes two machines: a UR5e on a table, built in MuJoCo from Universal Robots' own description, whose stop with a payload is its capability (`arm_mujoco.py`); and a heavy-duty mobile manipulator, synthetic, every number ours, whose arm sets what its base can do (`hdmm_mujoco.py`). The folder also holds a third machine, which the book does not report: Komatsu's PC138US-8, an excavator built from its brochure and an identification study and compared with the brochure's lifting table (`excavator_mujoco.py`, the package `excavator/`). Section 14.2 states, for the UR5e and the mobile manipulator, the labels that a declared payload allows, the actuators' limits, the constraints with their reserves, the recovery and the rest, and turns a lift of the mobile manipulator's arm into a demand and a turn into a limit on its speed; the plans hold the mobile manipulator's arm in one of four poses or lift it along one profile on a straight, and take a turn at a constant speed along the route's transitions; the test takes an allowance off its reserves for what its rigid model leaves out, counts in a turn the base's turning as well as its lateral acceleration, and counts the torque that the base's motion adds to the arm's 1.2 times; and the simulator, with the arm on its servos and every change of the base's braking or driving along a half-cosine over 0.6 s, shows the allowance to cover the braking limits, the turns and the lifts that it runs, a lift at its least distance (`labels_demand.py`). Section 14.3 governs the mobile manipulator with the allovi core of Chapter 10 across a yard of two turns, one on a wet patch, lifting its arm on the way and stopping at a wall; it shows, from bounds on how far a change of the state moves the rigid machine's zero-moment point and front-most point, that the levels that the test certifies are reserves in the sense of Definition 4.4, under an assumption on what the rigid machine leaves out (`hdmm/bounds.py`); it changes one module at a time, the capability predictor, the demand's announcement, the proposal, the control package and the governor itself, executes every plan that the governor kept unchanged in the simulator, and collects the conditions under which the machine loses its guarantees (`hdmm_governed.py`). Three scripts draw runs as videos (`arm_video.py`, `hdmm_video.py` and, for the excavator, `excavator_video.py`, below).

```
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python arm_mujoco.py                # writes results/arm-*
.venv/bin/python arm_mujoco.py --check        # also checks every value of its summary
.venv/bin/python hdmm_mujoco.py --check       # results/hdmm-*
.venv/bin/python excavator_mujoco.py --check  # results/excavator-*
.venv/bin/python labels_demand.py --check     # results/labels-*
.venv/bin/python hdmm_governed.py --check     # results/governed-*
```

The scripts need MuJoCo 3.16.0, NumPy, PyYAML (the arm) and SciPy (the excavator), as `requirements.txt` lists; they do not need ROS or xacro. They take about 3 s, 75 s, 45 s, eighteen minutes and ten minutes on the machine that wrote their shipped results (below). With `--check`, each script compares every value of its summary but the versions of the libraries with its `EXPECTED`, which holds among them every value that Section 14.1, 14.2 or 14.3 quotes from it (the book quotes none of the excavator's), and exits with an error if one differs. The check cannot confirm a value that a script takes from a published source, which it only repeats (`excavator/published.py` names the sources), and it does not compare the data files of the figures.

## What the scripts do

`arm_mujoco.py`

- writes **the record** of the experiment (`results/arm-record.txt`);
- checks **the description** against what UR publishes elsewhere: `tool0` against UR's table of Denavit-Hartenberg parameters over 200 random poses (seed 14), the links' masses and centres of mass against UR's table, the total against the datasheet; and records what MuJoCo Menagerie's model (commit `f3b6013`) holds instead;
- states **the task**: the carry pose, the base joint's angle against the task's, the loaded arm's first contact with the post, the empty arm's least clearance over the swing, and the clearance at the place;
- finds **the capability**: the largest deceleration of the base joint from 180 deg/s whose stop the joints' torques allow, empty and with 1, 2.5 and 5 kg, with the payload's centre of mass 0.10 and 0.15 m from the flange, and with Menagerie's armature, with the joint that binds, and each joint's own bound with 5 kg;
- simulates **the stops** with 5 kg from the place at full speed at 1, 1.2 and 2 times its largest deceleration and at the empty arm's, and the empty arm at its own (`results/arm-stops.csv`, Figure 14.1): for the base and the second wrist the largest torque asked and the updates clipped, the second and third wrists' departure from the pose and their speeds, the overrun if the base braked at its limit to rest, and the same stop with the other joints held in the pose (the base alone under the same controller, integrated exactly): its overrun, farthest angle, least clearance and the base's speed;
- repeats the stop at 1.2 times at **time steps** of 1, 0.5 and 0.25 ms, with the observed order of convergence and the overrun extrapolated to a zero time step.

`hdmm_mujoco.py`

- writes **the record** (`results/hdmm-record.txt`);
- computes, for the three poses and payloads of 0, 150 and 300 kg, the machine's centre of mass and the braking, accelerating and lateral accelerations at which a pair of wheels starts to lift, and the same with the arm slewed to the left at full reach;
- checks ten of them against **the simulated machine**: its settled wheel loads, extrapolated to zero, and the acceleration, raised slowly or applied at once to the machine at rest (once also with contacts four times stiffer), from which it tips over; the lateral acceleration that tips the loaded, slewed machine in steady turns of finite radius;
- finds what limits **the base's braking** on a ground of friction 0.6, the friction or the tipping, the payload above which tipping does, by pose, and the worst pose within the arm's ranges (`results/hdmm-braking.csv`, Figure 14.3);
- computes **the arm's loads**: the torques that hold the arm exactly in the carry pose with 300 kg at rest, while the base brakes at 3 m/s^2 and while it turns at 2 m/s^2.

`excavator_mujoco.py`

- writes **the record** (`results/excavator-record.txt`), with the source of every published number;
- states **what is published**, and the conflict between the brochure's operating weight and the web page's;
- fits **the front** and the arm cylinder, and checks that the stored values still come out; the cylinders' levers;
- fits **the masses**' one free parameter and compares the front with the identified parameters;
- compares the model with every entry of **the lifting table** at a fixed reach, the rows of the fit and the rows that test it, and says which limit binds (`results/excavator-lift-0.csv`, `excavator-lift-3.csv`);
- checks **the simulated machine** against the static model at the eight tipping-limited entries: its settled support forces, extrapolated to zero, and the load, raised slowly, from which it tips over.

`labels_demand.py`

- writes **the record** (`results/labels-record.txt`);
- finds **the UR5e's labels**: the declared interval's, with the share of 0.9 and without it, the nominal payload's, the empty arm's and the global capability's, with the joint that binds and the stop's angle; the bound against the payload's mass for four centres of mass (`results/labels-arm.csv`, Figure 14.4);
- finds **the UR5e's reserve**: the clearance where a stop from full speed at the place comes to rest, and the latest angle from which such a stop keeps the floor, under each label; the heaviest payloads that the global capability's label admits, and the largest share of a joint's torque that holds the arm at rest, up to the rated 5 kg and up to 7 kg, for the rest segment;
- simulates **the UR5e's stops** at the declared label for the nine payloads of the grid, for the worst corner from the latest angle, for four payloads outside the declaration, and for the worst corner under the empty arm's label from the place and from the latest angle that that label allows, with the joints clipped and their clipped updates;
- finds **the mobile manipulator's limits** by pose and label, both reserves less the allowance: the tipping reserve at rest, the braking limit and the speeds at which a plan may take turns of 3, 5 and 10 m, with what binds, also under the global capability, and checks that every lower speed of a turn holds too; the tipping reserve at rest under the global capability, its least value over the arm's ranges and the arm's torques at rest at full reach, for the rest;
- checks **the rigid model in the simulator**, the arm held on its servos: the centre of pressure at every braking limit of the four poses, empty, declared and under the global capability, the deceleration raised over 5 s (declared) and with the rise, braking from cruising and from the drive and stopping, against the rigid machine's zero-moment point at every instant, with the servos' largest torques; a braking at once, for comparison; the turns of 3, 5 and 10 m at their speeds in the four poses under the three labels, each label with its heaviest payload, through the turn, braking in it from the middle of its entry, its arc and its exit and from where the test binds, and driving off in it from rest where the test binds, the machine held in the base's turning frame; MuJoCo's ratios of frictional to normal impedance of 5, 10, 20 and 50, and the largest change of a shortfall between 20 and 50; the base's creep at the default ratio, 1, and at 20; on the labels' least friction, the base's creep and the front-most point's excess over the rigid machine's in the stops, and the base's creep and turn on its contacts in the turns; the arm's holding torques at the braking limits;
- turns **a lift** into a demand: the tipping reserve that it consumes in the static model, the requirement, a plan in force that holds it, within the forecast horizon and beyond it, and the reserve that a plan that begins the lift then keeps; the plan in force simulated braking with the rise; the simulated lift of 4 s at rest, and with the base braking with the rise from the drive, at the requirement's limit and at the end pose's own, and once from cruising, from onsets every 0.1 s up to 6 s and every 0.02 s from 3.2 to 4.4 s, on servos offset against gravity and on servos not offset, with 297 kg within the horizon and 300 kg beyond it, against the rigid machine's least along the lift and its distance where a shortfall at an instant exceeds the allowance, with the servos' torques, the front-most point and the base's travel; the stops at the top speed with the rise; the clearance that it consumes; the tipping reserve against the recovery's deceleration (`results/labels-reserve.csv`, Figure 14.7);
- finds what **a turn** allows: the speed by pose and radius, with what binds and its lateral acceleration, and the distance and time in which to slow to it, the rise included; a stop in a turn at 0.5 m/s^2; the speeds of turns to either side reaching left, and the largest level that a demand for a turn can ask, with the speeds at it;
- finds **the allowance's** cover: the largest shortfall of a simulated plan's least distance below the rigid machine's, and the largest at a single instant with the arm held and during a lift; the servos' largest torques; the base's largest creep and the front-most point's largest excess on the labels' least friction.

`hdmm_governed.py`

- writes **the record** (`results/governed-record.txt`);
- states **the limits** that the test uses for the labels of the runs: by pose and label, the braking limit carrying, reaching and lifting at the floor and at the lift's requirement for the label's payloads, and the speed of the yard's turns, with what binds; the lift's requirement for the declared and the misdeclared payload, with the package's allowance before a lift's end, and the check of the assumption of reserve consumption with it;
- runs **the governed machine** along the yard in ten configurations, the reference and nine that change one module (two in one), and the cruise proposal without the governor, each for 100 periods: from when it stays at rest in the drop zone, and when it first rests there, when the lift begins and whether it is overloaded, the speeds in the turns, the simulated centre of pressure's least distance inside the contacts against the rigid model's and above the level in force, the shortfalls against the allowances, the arm's torques and where the slew's is largest, the base's creep, the wheels in contact, the arm's deviation from its planned pose held and along and across the lift's path, the governor's interventions, the invalid labels and where they lie, and the alarms (`results/governed-trace-*.csv`, `governed-route.csv`, Figures 14.8, 14.11 and 14.12);
- probes **the lead time**, the lift announced one period before its onset and at it;
- for **the map without the wet patch**, the share of an axle's load that the rigid machine's ground must supply in the second turn, against the patch's true friction, and the worst recovery kept there, simulated;
- runs **the checks of Section 10.3** on the reference, on the map without the wet patch and on the misdeclared payload;
- replays the executed laws of the reference run and of the map without the wet patch on a fresh machine and reads **each wheel's contact force** in the second turn: each wheel's and the rear axle's largest ratio of tangential to normal force, and the steps at which a wheel's contact lies on its friction cone;
- finds what **the funnel's bounds on the arm** can cost at the states that it evaluates: the worst corner of the bounds, held and about a pose of a lift's path, on the zero-moment point's distance and on the front-most point; each joint's largest change per radian of the front-most point's distance from the stop line and of the zero-moment point's from the rectangle's edge; each joint's largest move of the zero-moment point itself per radian, on a straight and at the turns' states, and the largest change of its distance at a single state of a turn; which edge lies nearest the point and how much farther the side edges lie; the front-most point's own move per radian and its part along the route; the largest change per radian of a turn pair's level, the least over one period's states; the worst corner's cost at a single state of the turns; and, at full reach with 297 kg, the point's sideways move for the funnel's bound on the slew against the slew's term in the tipping norm;
- states **the norms and the reserves** (`hdmm/bounds.py`, Section 14.3.2, Table 14.5): the zero-moment point's closed form against MuJoCo's inverse dynamics; the levels' grid and the route's largest curvature and its rates; the constants of Lemma 14.1 for the clearance and for the margin against tipping over, and that every weight of the two norms is at least its constant; the largest move of the zero-moment point and the largest change of its distance per unit of each component at the states evaluated, and that every rate measured, here and in the funnel's bounds on the arm, lies below its constant; for the payload intervals of the runs' labels, at the largest speeds at which the test lets a plan take the yard's turns under each, what the funnel's spread about the states that the test evaluates and the test's sampling of them can cost the front edge's distance, and the side and the rear edges' least distance against the front edge's largest (Proposition 14.2); the gaps between the poses that the test takes along a lift's path; the room beyond the level that all this leaves, the arm held and before a lift's end; and how much farther forward than the point that the test takes at the rest the front-most point can lie, with the room beyond the clearance's floor;
- executes **every plan in force unchanged** in five configurations, the reference with 243 and 297 kg, the gentle package and the route-aware proposal with 297 kg and the fixed predictor with 300 kg: each plan's head, recovery and rest from the simulated state at its period's start, the least simulated distance above each pair's profile entry and the rest's level, the shortfalls held and before a lift's end and the part of them at the arm's actual pose, and the arm's deviation;
- states **the funnel and the allowances** over the runs with valid labels and every plan executed unchanged, and tests Assumption 14.3 there: the largest shortfall at the arm's actual pose against the room that Proposition 14.2 leaves.

Every limit and reserve that `labels_demand.py` prints is rounded down, and every stop, consumption, shortfall, travel and torque rounded up, at the digits printed; `hdmm_governed.py` rounds every limit, reserve, distance and level down and every share, ratio, shortfall, creep, deviation and torque up.

## The videos

Three scripts draw runs of this chapter as videos, with the package `../render`, whose README gives what it needs besides this chapter's requirements: Pillow, EGL and ffmpeg. Each runs its configuration again with this chapter's modules unchanged, records the simulated state while MuJoCo steps (it copies the state after the steps and changes nothing in them), and draws the record; the drawing simulates nothing, and no result above changes. `--quick` draws a small video, without stills; `--export DIR` writes the scene and frames for the path tracer of `../render/cycles` instead.

```
.venv/bin/pip install pillow
.venv/bin/python excavator_video.py        # results/video/excavator-*
.venv/bin/python arm_video.py --ur DIR     # results/video/ur5e-*
.venv/bin/python hdmm_video.py             # results/video/hdmm-*
```

- `excavator_video.py` runs `excavator.model.onset()` for the lifting table's entry with the hook at the ground and the reach of 4.6 m over the front, records every run that it simulates at 30 frames per second, with the supports' forces and the force at the lift point, and checks onset's result against `results/excavator-summary.txt` (5789.5 kg). It draws the first two runs, those of the bisection's bracket: at 98 % of the static tipping load the machine stands, its rear supports at 0.6 kN each; at 102 % they carry nothing once the load passes the static tipping load, and the machine tips over the front tipping line. The drawing of the second run stops at a lean of 15 deg, since the model has no contacts but its four supports and keeps turning through the ground. The machine is drawn on the model's bodies to the brochure's dimensions where it gives them and ours where not, its cylinders at the model's lengths in the pose between pins of ours; the load, a force in the simulation, is drawn as a bag that fills with water as the force rises, hanging in a pit; the supports' forces are arrows beside the tracks. The pit, its shoring and the yard are scenery; the script checks that the camera keeps 1.5 m from the scenery and sees the machine in every frame. About twenty minutes on two processes at 1920 x 1080.

- `arm_video.py` runs the two stops of Table S14.2 from the latest angles, one of which ends 0.0100 m short of the post and the other in it: the heaviest corner of the declared interval, 4.95 kg with its centre of mass 0.075 m below the flange, swinging at 180 deg/s and stopped under the declared label from the latest angle that it allows, -61.072 deg, and under the empty arm's label from the latest angle that this label allows with the box in place, -63.857 deg. It records the state after every step of 0.5 ms, with the force of the post's contacts, and draws one frame per millisecond, so that the video runs at 1/33 of real time: each stop's first state held while the camera moves in, the stop, and the run's last state held. The arm is drawn with the visual meshes of UR's description, from a checkout DIR of `Universal_Robots_ROS2_Description` at commit `7ce98d8`, the commit of `arm/description/`, placed where the description places them; the post and the payload are the model's own boxes, and the clearance's floor of 0.01 m is drawn as a translucent slab against the post's face; the workbench, the tote at the pick, the plate under the place, the fence and the hall are scenery. About seventeen minutes on two processes at 1920 x 1080.
- `hdmm_video.py` runs the reference configuration of `hdmm_governed.py` and records the state at 30 frames per second of simulated time. The simulation, as Section 14.3's, holds the machine in the frame of the base's commanded motion along the route; the drawing places that frame on the yard at each frame's commanded arc length and the simulated bodies in it, the base with its creep, pitch and roll on its contacts and the arm on its servos. Every part of the machine is drawn on those bodies to dimensions of ours: the model has four contact spheres instead of wheels, a box for its base, capsules for its boom and stick and a cube for its tool, none of them drawn; the drawn wheels turn and steer as the commanded motion would turn them. The yard is the route of `hdmm/route.py`, with its dashed centre line, the wet patch around the second turn, the wall at the stop line and the drop zone within 0.5 m of it; the edge lines, the containers, the pallets, the cones and the warehouses are scenery. Before it draws, the script checks that the camera keeps 2 m from the scenery and sees the machine in every frame. About forty minutes on two processes at 1920 x 1080.

## Provenance and reproducibility

The UR5e's files are Universal Robots' (BSD 3-Clause, `arm/description/ur_description/LICENSE`); every other file is ours. The excavator's published numbers are in `excavator/published.py` with their sources; the brochure, the web page, the paper and the standards' sample pages are not copied here. The heavy-duty mobile manipulator is synthetic and its numbers are those of no machine.

MuJoCo computes in double precision. The shipped results were produced on one x86-64 Linux machine (a 2.10 GHz Xeon), with MuJoCo 3.16.0 built from its source at the tag 3.16.0 (commit `a892750`) by clang 18.1.3, under CPython 3.13.17 with NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1 and PyYAML 6.0.3; repeated runs wrote identical files. The previous version of `hdmm_governed.py`, whose norm and alarm check differed, wrote the same data files, and the same value wherever its summary and this one's share a key; it also ran on a second machine (a 2.80 GHz Xeon) and wrote there every value and data file that it wrote on the first. They have not been reproduced with other versions, nor the other scripts on another machine; there the floating-point results may differ in the last digits, and the check reports any value that differs at the precision printed.
