Record of the experiment of Section 14.1: a synthetic heavy-duty mobile manipulator, how its arm sets what its base can do

model and asset provenance
  synthetic, every number ours (hdmm/model.py); no published model of such a machine was found; the payload range after Bahari et al. (2025); the arm's ranges ours: 0 to 60, -150 to 0; at least 0.2
  base 3500; 0, 0, 0.75; +-1.1, +-0.85; arm 0.6, 1.3; 2.4, 2; 350, 200; tool, payload 100; 300
simulator and solver
  MuJoCo 3.16.0; NumPy and Python 2.5.3, 3.13.17; 0.001; implicitfast; elliptic; contacts' softness (MuJoCo's defaults) solref, solimp 0.02, 1; 0.9, 0.95, 0.001, 0.5, 2; also solref 0.005, 1 for one comparison; the contacts' ratio of frictional to normal impedance 20, which keeps the machine's creep on them small (Section 14.2, labels_demand.py)
time steps
  1 ms; the extrapolated loads after 2 s at rest; the onset runs settle 2 s at rest, then raise the acceleration over 5 s (or at once) and hold it 24 s; tipped if the base then leans more than 0.1 deg
actuators and sensors
  the arm rigid in its pose; the torques that hold it exactly in its pose computed on a fixed base under the tilted gravity; no sensors: the state from the simulator
frames and units
  SI units; the base's x forward, y left, z up, the ground at z = 0
uncertainty bounds
  none: the machine's numbers are exact by construction; the ground's friction 0.6 (ours); 1.5 in the simulated checks, so that nothing slides
constraints
  all four wheels on the ground (the margin against tipping)
terminal behaviour
  none: Section 14.1 measures limits
training and evaluation split
  none: nothing learns in Section 14.1
seeds
  none: no random numbers
hardware
  Linux x86_64, Intel(R) Xeon(R) Processor @ 2.10GHz
guarantee status
  none: a synthetic machine; the limits are those of its model, not of any machine
