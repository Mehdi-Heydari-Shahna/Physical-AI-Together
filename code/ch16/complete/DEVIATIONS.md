# Deviations from the protocol of the complete case

Recorded as they occur; this file is not part of the frozen record.

1. **Identification, first start (29 October 2026, 09:54 UTC).** The first start of `complete_identify.py` stopped after 5.7 minutes with an error of its own scripted excitation: in one identification world the script, after its maneuvers on the slope, would have unfolded the tool with the cart at rest at 23.97 m, and its safety check (no reference beyond 24.6 m with the reach) refused the period. The script wrote nothing; no bound, summary or count was produced or looked at. The excitation now first backs the cart away to rest at or before 23.2 m before it unfolds the tool (`Script.back_to`); nothing else changed. The scripts are not part of the frozen record, so the frozen modules and this protocol are unchanged, and the identification was started again.

2. **The docstring of `Guard` in `../../ch10/allovi/governor.py`, corrected before the evaluation (29 October 2026).** After the bounds were frozen and the offset calibrated, while the learners' training ran (its processes had loaded the module before the change), and before any run of the evaluation, real-time or stress phase, the docstring's claim that replacing a failed output by its default "never weakens a guarantee" was qualified: the guarantees hold for the defaults provided that the common steps still finish before the deadline (Section 8.3), which a reviewer had found contradicted by the book. No code changed. `FROZEN.json` now holds the new hash of the file and, under `superseded`, the hash that the identification ran with (`0b4ea4e8...4846164`).

3. **Two wordings of the scripts, found after the evaluation (29 October 2026).** Not departures from the protocol, but errata of the scripts' text, which are left unchanged so that their recorded hashes stay those of the runs. In `results/complete-identification.txt`, written by `complete_identify.py`, the line "periods in which the tool unfolds = 400" counts the unfoldings of the tool (four in each of the 40 excitation runs, and the governed runs' unfoldings), not periods. And the docstring of `complete_evaluate.py` names a file `results/complete-record.txt` that the script does not write: the record of the run is the section `[record]` of `results/complete-summary.txt`.
4. **The diagnosis, written after the evaluation (29 October 2026).** `complete_diagnose.py` was written after the evaluation's results were known, to explain the failures of H3 and H4 and the paired difference between F-preview and A-rule. It reruns runs of the evaluation offline, checks them against `results/complete-runs.csv`, and tests nothing; its findings are explanations, not results under the protocol. It runs after the stress phase, so that the real-time and stress phases ran as the protocol ordered them. After the timed phases, two parts were added to it: part [3] reads the real-time records (no rerun), and part [4] reruns the 60 runs of the stress test S1 to see where the cart went after its alarms. Part [3] was first run at 13:01:58 UTC, reading results only, while the offline part of the stress phase ran.
5. **The real-time phase in two invocations (decided before the phase began).** `complete_realtime.py` ran as two invocations, `--part 1` (seeds 45001 to 45010) and `--part 2` (45011 to 45020), and `--merge` combined their files, because a job on the machine used is limited to two hours. Each run is the same as in a single invocation; only the processes are started anew between the halves.
6. **Other work on the machine during the timed phases (29 October 2026).** The protocol asks that one run at a time use the machine, with no other work on it. No other experiment ran during the real-time phase (10:59:50 to 12:00:35 and 12:00:43 to 13:01:33 UTC) or the timed stress tests S4 and S5 (about 13:08 to 13:29 UTC), but the session that carried out the protocol edited text files and ran short commands on the same machine during parts of the real-time phase: before 11:27 UTC (text edits and short commands, whose times the session's kept log does not show); from 11:26:53 to 11:35:23 UTC (edits, file reads, searches, and one Python command of under a second at 11:34:58); and from 12:00:38 to 12:03:43 UTC (reads, edits and one search). It ran nothing from 11:35:23 to 12:00:38, from 12:03:43 to 13:01:37, and during S4 and S5 (its last command before them at 13:06:20, the next at 13:29:07). From the runs' recorded durations, seven of the 14 real-time runs that differed from their offline runs ran while it ran nothing, among them the run with the longest delay (F-preview, tube, world 45010, at about 11:56 UTC); one (A-learner, tube, world 45006, at about 11:31:43) coincided with a short command; the other six ran before 11:27 UTC. The records do not identify the cause of any delay.
7. **Errata and one definition, found after the timed phases (29 October 2026).** The files are left unchanged, so that their recorded hashes stay those of the runs.
   - The docstring of `realtime.py` says that the plant computes a period in about 4 ms; on the machine of the runs it took 7.9 ms at the median and up to 98.6 ms (`../results/complete-realtime-summary.txt`).
   - The plant executes the plan committed for a period if the plan has arrived when the plant reaches the period's boundary (`realtime.py`, `plant_main`), not, as this protocol and the docstring say, if it arrived before the boundary. The two differ only when the plant itself is late. Of the 20 plans that the governor sent after their boundary, 10 arrived before the plant, itself late by 7 to 257 ms, reached the boundary, and were executed; the other 10 are the "periods whose plan arrived after the boundary (fallback executed)" of `../results/complete-realtime-summary.txt` (`../results/complete-diagnosis.txt`, part [3]). H6 fails under either reading.
   - The docstring of `../complete_stress.py` says that the script takes about fifteen minutes; it took 27 minutes.
